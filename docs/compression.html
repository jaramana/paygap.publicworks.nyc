Chapter four

Experience stopped paying

Salary compression is when the pay gap between a new hire and a long-serving colleague narrows to nothing. It is one of the most common grievances in public sector bargaining and one of the least documented. The City payroll records a start date for almost every employee, so it can be measured.

Where a decade buys the least

Each row compares the median base salary of people hired in the last two years against colleagues in the same title with more than ten years at the agency. Where the two dots nearly touch, experience is worth nothing in salary terms.

Read this carefully, it has a real limitation. Tenure here is time at the current agency, not time in public service. Someone with twenty years at another agency who transferred last year counts as a new hire. That makes these figures a floor: real compression is probably somewhat less severe than the raw comparison suggests in titles where transfers are common, and about right where they are not.

A ratio near 1.0 is also not automatically bad. Some titles are flat by design, with a single negotiated rate and no seniority steps at all. What the number tells you is whether staying produces a raise, not whether the pay is fair.
