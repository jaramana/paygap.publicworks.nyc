Chapter six

The workers nobody counts

Every other page on this site covers salaried staff. That leaves out millions of records: school crossing guards, lunch helpers, college aides, seasonal parks staff and election-day poll workers, paid by the hour or the day. Earlier versions of this site excluded all of them. They should not have.

This roster is stranger than it looks, and the strangeness is the finding. Roughly half of the hourly roster carries a base rate of exactly $1.00. Almost all of those are election workers, who are paid a flat stipend per election day rather than an hourly wage, with the payroll system storing a placeholder. Around three quarters of hourly records show zero regular hours for the year. Averaging the roster as a whole produces a number that describes nobody, so this page separates the placeholder rows out and reports the continuously employed separately.

What the hourly rate actually is

For those who work a real year

Restricting to people with at least 1,000 regular hours, which is roughly half a full-time year, gives a population for whom an annual figure means something.
