How the numbers are built

Fiscal years

City fiscal years run 1 July to 30 June, so FY2025 covers July 2024 through June 2025. Inflation adjustment uses the twelve-month average of the New York-area consumer price index across each fiscal year rather than a single month's reading.

Prices and rent

Prices are the BLS consumer price index for all urban consumers in the New York-Newark-Jersey City area, series CUURS12ASA0. Rent is the same programme's index for rent of primary residence, CUURS12ASEHA. A national index would understate what New Yorkers paid, so it is not used. Zillow's rent index appears alongside in dollar terms; the difference between the two measures is explained on the citywide page.

Who is counted

Salary figures cover employees paid per annum with an active leave status at 30 June. Hourly and day-rate workers are analysed separately, because a rate and a salary are different quantities and averaging them together produces nonsense.

Medians and means

Salary distributions are right-skewed, so the mean sits above what a typical jobholder earns. Both are available; the median leads.

Small groups

Any group with fewer than 30 people has its statistics withheld. A three-person group produces a spectacular-looking figure that is really one person's salary. Suppressed rows stay in the downloads with a flag, so "too small to report" is distinguishable from "not in the data".

Above that floor, the minimum group size used in rankings is a control on the Compare page rather than a fixed rule, because the right threshold depends on what you are asking.

Gender

The payroll does not record gender. Estimates come from matching first names against Social Security Administration birth registrations from 1880 onward, weighting each person by the proportion of that name registered male or female. Names with fewer than 25 registrations are excluded, since a handful of records would swing the weight to a false certainty.

What this cannot see

  • Gender identity. The name proxy estimates sex recorded at birth. It cannot see transgender or non-binary people, and it says nothing reliable about any individual.
  • About one employee in ten. Name matching covers roughly 89% of the workforce, and that share falls slightly each year as the workforce diversifies.
  • Race and ethnicity. Not recorded in the payroll. The name-origin comparison is a crude proxy for national origin and should be read as one.
  • Total public service. Tenure is time at the current agency, so anyone who transferred between agencies reads as newer than they are. Compression and tenure figures are conservative as a result.
  • Why anyone left. The CEASED status mixes quits, retirements, seasonal endings, layoffs and deaths.
  • Whether overtime was chosen. Hours and dollars are recorded; mandated and volunteered shifts look identical.
  • Take-home pay. Everything here is gross, before tax, pension and health contributions.
  • Whether pay is fair. This measures what people are paid, not what the work is worth. No dataset settles the second question.
