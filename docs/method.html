Method

This page tells you where each figure comes from, how this site calculates it, what it cannot show, and how to get the data.

Scope

This site shows the payroll records of the City of New York. It gives pay, overtime, and tenure for each civil service title and each agency.

The City publishes these records. This site does not collect new data. It calculates statistics from the published records.

Sources

  • NYC Citywide Payroll Data (Fiscal Year), NYC Open Data
  • Consumer Price Index, US Bureau of Labor Statistics, New York area series
  • Zillow Observed Rent Index, New York metro
  • Popular Baby Names, Social Security Administration
  • Local Law 11 of 2012, the NYC Open Data Law
  • Pendleton Civil Service Reform Act, 1883, US National Archives

Calculation

Fiscal years

A City fiscal year starts on July 1 and ends on June 30. Fiscal year 2025 is July 2024 to June 2025.

This site adjusts for inflation with the average consumer price index for the 12 months of each fiscal year. It does not use the index for one month.

Prices and rent

For prices, this site uses series CUURS12ASA0 from the Bureau of Labor Statistics. That series covers all urban consumers in the New York-Newark-Jersey City area.

For rent, it uses series CUURS12ASEHA. That series covers rent of a primary residence.

A national index is too low for New York. Thus this site does not use one. It also shows the Zillow rent index in dollars. The citywide page gives the difference between the two measures.

Who is counted

Salary figures include each employee with an annual salary and an active leave status on June 30.

This site calculates hourly workers and day-rate workers separately. A rate and a salary are different quantities. An average of the two quantities has no meaning.

Medians and means

Salary distributions are not symmetrical. A small number of high salaries pulls the mean up. Thus the mean is higher than the pay of a typical employee.

This site shows the median first. It also gives the mean.

Small groups

If a group has less than 30 persons, this site does not show its statistics. In a group of three persons, one salary controls the result.

Above 30 persons, you set the minimum group size. It is a control on the compare page. The correct minimum is different for each question.

Gender

The payroll does not record gender. This site estimates gender from first names.

It compares each first name with the birth records of the Social Security Administration from 1880. It then gives each person a weight. The weight is the proportion of that name that is male or female.

If a name has less than 25 birth records, this site does not use it. Too few records give a false certainty.

Absences

An empty value is not a zero. It shows that this site does not have the value.

Each file has an n column. That column gives the number of persons in the group.

Each file also has a suppressed column. That column is TRUE if the group has less than 30 persons. A suppressed row keeps its name and its headcount, but its statistics are empty. Thus you can tell the difference between "too small to report" and "not in the data".

A column name that starts with real_ is adjusted for inflation to the most recent fiscal year. A plain salary column is in the dollars of its own year.

Limits

This site cannot show you these things.

  • Gender identity. The name proxy estimates the sex in the birth record. It cannot identify a transgender or non-binary person. It is not reliable for one person.
  • About one employee in ten. The name match covers about 89 percent of the workforce. That share becomes smaller each year as the workforce becomes more diverse.
  • Race and ethnicity. The payroll does not record them. The name origin comparison is a weak proxy for national origin. Read it as one.
  • Total public service. Tenure is the time at the current agency. An employee who moved between agencies looks more recent than that employee is. Thus the compression figures and the tenure figures are low.
  • The reason a person left. The CEASED status includes a resignation, a retirement, the end of seasonal work, a layoff, and a death.
  • The reason for overtime. The records give hours and dollars. Required overtime and voluntary overtime look the same.
  • Take-home pay. Each figure is gross pay, before tax, pension, and health contributions.
  • Fair pay. This site measures what the City pays. It does not measure what the work is worth. No dataset answers that question.

Downloads

Each file is plain CSV. The files hold real numbers, not formatted text. Thus they open in a spreadsheet or a script without more work.

Build

The pipeline is five R scripts and one entry point. Clone the repository, then run this command: 

Rscript run.R

The first run downloads about 12 years of payroll data. It takes about 20 minutes. After the first run, the pipeline downloads only the most recent fiscal year.

The file R/00_config.R holds each threshold, the base year for inflation, and the suppression floor. Change a value in that file, then build the site again.

Reuse

The source code is on GitHub with a BSD 3-Clause license. The data is free to use, but you must give attribution. You do not need permission. Give credit to NYC Open Data for the payroll.
