Fiscal years 2014 to 2025 · 288,000 employees

City pay went up.
Rent went up more.

Since 2014 the median salary at the City of New York has risen 37.8%. New York inflation over the same period was 31.5%, so pay is modestly ahead of prices. Rent rose 38.8%. This site works out what that means for each of the City's 1,500 civil service titles, and shows its work.

Read this the right way. The pay line is the median across everyone the City employed that year, not a raise. When the City hires many people into lower-paid titles, the median falls even if nobody's salary was cut. To follow an actual job over time, use your title.

Built for the people in the data

This started because working out one title's real pay history, by hand, turned out to be genuinely useful in a room full of colleagues trying to make a case. That is the whole idea here: the City publishes the payroll, but publishing is not the same as making something usable.

Every figure can be traced. The data is downloadable as plain CSV with a data dictionary, the code is public, and where the method is weak the page says so next to the number rather than in a footnote.
